# Qualified custodian and SOC reports

**In one sentence.** In the US, an investment adviser must keep client assets with a qualified
custodian, and a custodian's controls are evidenced by SOC reports, auditors' reports on a service
organisation's controls.

## The problem

A client, and the client's auditors, need to know that the custodian may hold the assets and that
its controls exist and work. Neither question is answered by the custodian's own description.

## The idea

Under the US Investment Advisers Act custody rule (Rule 206(4)-2), an adviser keeps client funds and
securities with a qualified custodian: a bank or savings association, a registered broker-dealer, a
futures commission merchant, or certain foreign financial institutions.

A SOC report is an auditor's report on a service organisation's controls: SOC 1 for controls relevant
to clients' financial reporting (ISAE 3402 internationally), SOC 2 for the AICPA Trust Services
Criteria (security required; availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, privacy optional).
Type I covers design at a date; Type II also covers operation over a period.

## Why custody cares

Qualification decides who may hold advisers' client assets. SOC reports are the evidence clients and
their auditors ask for. Neither shows that the assets exist: that is a financial audit or a proof of
reserves (chapter 6).

## In the demo

The policy engine and audit log are controls a SOC 2 report would test: denials with reasons,
approvals with approver identities, and the anchored audit head. The demo has no period of operating
evidence.
