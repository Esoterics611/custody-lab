# Qualified custodian and SOC reports

**Definition.** Under the US Investment Advisers Act custody rule (Rule 206(4)-2), an adviser
keeps client funds and securities with a qualified custodian: a bank or savings association, a
registered broker-dealer, a futures commission merchant, or certain foreign financial
institutions. A SOC report is an auditor's report on a service organisation's controls: SOC 1 for
controls relevant to clients' financial reporting (ISAE 3402 internationally), SOC 2 for the AICPA
Trust Services Criteria (security required; availability, processing integrity, confidentiality,
privacy optional). Type I covers design at a date; Type II also covers operation over a period.

**Why custody cares.** Qualification decides who may hold advisers' client assets. SOC reports
are the evidence clients and their auditors ask for. Neither shows that the assets exist: that is
a financial audit or a proof of reserves.

**In the demo.** The policy engine and audit log are controls a SOC 2 report would test; the demo
has no period of operating evidence.
